# Research plan for discovering useful latent subgoals

**Can a world model discover intermediate goals in latent space that improve final task success when pursued?** The planner receives the public observation and action history, the terminal goal and the remaining action budget. It learns from unsegmented trajectories and from its own attempts, with no hand-labeled intermediate goals, no hand-set arrival test and no fixed replanning schedule.

An intermediate goal is a latent vector `z` sampled from the world model. One executor conditioned on `z` pursues it for at most `k` actions, which makes it an option. Its meaning is where pursuing it lands, so no decoder target or arrival test is needed. A budget-conditioned option value scores each candidate with its duration, given the terminal goal and the remaining budget, and the encoded terminal goal is always one of the candidates. The value is the terminal success that pursuing the option leads to under the actual budget, minus a cost per action, so it includes the time the option spends and any recovery after it fails.

The project first defined a goal as a region of states given by a generative decoder `p(s | z)`. Section 4 found that region goals did not beat point goals in any environment, while choosing which target to pursue decided whether targets helped at all. The decoder therefore left the method.

## Hypotheses

1. **Goal-dependent value.** From a matched start, pursuing some intermediate goals improves terminal success under a shared executor and budget, and changing the terminal goal changes which goals help.
2. **Plausibility differs from value.** A world model proposes useful candidates, but its frozen energy, its prediction error and how often a goal appears in demonstrations identify plausible futures rather than valuable ones. A value learned from outcomes identifies the valuable ones before rollout.
3. **The budget changes the choice.** The goal worth reaching, and how long to pursue it, change with the remaining budget. A completion conditioned on the budget but chosen without a time cost spends the whole budget.
4. **One option value can replace hand-set structure.** Choosing the candidate, its duration and when to switch with one budget-conditioned value, with the terminal goal as a candidate, beats direct execution and fixed-horizon subgoals without arrival tests or replanning schedules.
5. **Composition and depth.** Separately proposed goals can be recombined into routes that demonstrations rarely contain, and the advantage of choosing goals with a value grows with the number of dependent decisions.

## Method

| Component | Design |
| --- | --- |
| World model | The masked completion model predicts latents `ẑ(h, g, b, q)` at query offsets `q` from the public history `h`, terminal condition `g` and budget `b`, with a frozen reconstruction codec. A residual flow samples several latent futures per offset. |
| Latent-goal executor | One GCIVL policy whose goals are frozen encodings of archive observations. It pursues sampled latents and the encoded terminal goal alike. |
| Option value | `Q(h, g, z, k, b, λ)` and `V(h, g, b, λ)` on latent features with several heads on bootstrap masks. Hindsight options from the archive give in-sample semi-Markov expectile backups: `1 − λd` on reaching the goal after `d` actions, `−λb` when the budget runs out, and `−λk + V` at the landing state otherwise, with `V` clipped to the returns still possible. The time cost `λ` is a value input. |
| Online correction | Rounds of the planner on training resets, exploring uniformly over proposal budgets, give the executor's attempts; each reset is also played by uninterrupted pursuit of the terminal goal, so the goal option is labeled by its own outcome. The value continues training on batches mixing attempts and hindsight options. Evaluation keeps interruption. |
| Planner | Candidates are flow samples at every query offset, proposed under the remaining budget and under shorter budgets, plus the encoded terminal goal. The planner pursues the highest-value candidate, switches among cached candidates when one is better by more than the ensemble spread, and samples again when the duration runs out, the value drops or the encoded observation leaves the predicted path. |

The controlled tasks in sections 1 to 3 used an earlier pipeline: each latent proposal was decoded to one target, projected to a cell, a graph node or an observation, and scored with Monte Carlo selectors on hand-designed features.

## Positioning against prior work

No single component of the method is new. Director, HIQL, SAGE, HWM, H-JEPA, WorldDP and HDFlow already propose latent subgoals for a latent-conditioned executor. HiTS already attaches a duration to a subgoal and continues after a miss. Horizon-aware C-Learning and TDM already learn horizon-conditioned reaching values. Options, semi-Markov backups and interruption go back to Sutton, Precup and Singh (1999), and RDM already replans diffusion plans on demand. The contribution is how a latent goal is chosen: one value learned from outcomes and the executor's attempts decides the candidate, its duration and when to switch, under the global remaining budget, with the terminal goal always competing.

| One budget-conditioned value chooses the candidate, its duration and when to switch, with the goal as a candidate. | HiTS optimizes discounted return with a per-subgoal duration. ## Starting evidence

- In the 391-action Maze2D Medium pilot, direct tracking succeeded on 23 of 64 starts. Among its 41 failures, retrospective inspection found at least one candidate that arrived and then succeeded on 15 and 10 starts for the two checkpoints. Selecting the lowest conditional flow energy before rollout yielded 25 and 18 successes, respectively, tying the highest-energy rule within each checkpoint. Better decoded trajectory error and candidate coverage motivate further testing of selection. [Source and protocol](experiments.md#test-latent-energy-against-terminal-success)

An executor following public geometry provides a local control for the matched attempts.

### 4. Compare region goals with decoded point goals

This was E1. On the same latent proposals it compared a decoded point goal with exact arrival, a latent point with encoder-consistency arrival `‖enc(s) − z‖ ≤ ε` in the style of SAGE and HWM, a region goal with region-support arrival `log p(s | z) ≥ κ` under a learned decoder, and a Gaussian region in the style of DisCo RL. Arrival thresholds were calibrated to equal recall on development windows. Maze2D and Scene ran in closed loop, replanning at fixed fractions of the remaining budget and handing over to the terminal executor in the last eight actions. [Maze2D protocol](experiments.md#compare-region-goals-with-point-goals-in-maze2d) · [Scene protocol](experiments.md#compare-region-goals-with-point-goals-in-scene)

**Development result in the dependency grid (provisional).** Five model seeds on the 16 development layouts, averaging terminal success over the eight flow candidates per start, with 95% intervals that resample layouts after averaging seeds. Judging arrival on the whole observation instead of the decoded cell gave the largest effect. With the same observation executor and decoded target, region-support arrival beat exact-cell arrival by 0.21 [0.15, 0.29] at depth one and by 0.16 [0.12, 0.21] at depth two at recall 0.9, on every seed. Exact-cell arrival ignores the key flags, so it hands control to the key-blind continuation before the key is collected. Under region-support arrival, a sampled realization beat the decoded mean by 0.02 to 0.03 on four of five seeds. At depth two the flow decoder beat the matched Gaussian by 0.01 to 0.05. Region support did not beat encoder consistency at equal recall: at recall 0.9 encoder consistency was ahead by 0.024 [0.014, 0.034] at depth two. Regions were not coherent: the variance of value across realizations of one region (0.085 at depth two) exceeded the variance between regions of the same start (0.058). Depth-two success rose with recall up to 0.9 and fell slightly at 0.95. The learned executor already encodes the demonstrated key-fetching behavior, so depth one barely depends on the arrival rule. The grid therefore cannot separate goal forms well. The next tests move to Maze2D and Scene. The runs are under `results/dependency-grid-regions-dev.Ya1JrG/`.

A first one-shot Maze2D run, which declared one target per start, could not separate goal forms because a perfect shortest-path waypoint added at most 0.03 over direct tracking. It is withdrawn as evidence and kept under `results/pointmaze-regions-dev.mPhg3O/`.

**Maze2D closed-loop result (development).** Three model seeds on 64 development starts per layout, comparing the original models with models trained at four times the width on 200,000 windows. Direct tracking reached 0.58 in Medium and 0.48 in Large, and the shortest-path oracle reached 1.00 in both. The larger models helped most: in Large the original models stayed between 0.02 and 0.17, below direct tracking, and the larger ones reached 0.39 to 0.58. In Medium, decoded targets with native arrival reached 1.00 under the tracker, and decoded targets with region-support arrival at recall 0.9 reached 0.995. Region goals did not beat point goals. Region-support arrival at best matched native arrival, and the arms with the latent executor fell 0.15 to 0.35 below decoded targets in Medium, mostly because that executor is less accurate than the tracker. In Large, seed variance dominated: two seeds beat direct tracking by up to 0.38 while the third failed on nearly every start, even though offline prediction error was the same for all three. That seed reached its own targets but circled without approaching the goal, so offline prediction error does not predict closed-loop success. Every arm with model targets also arrived late. Successes came at about step 585 of 600 in Medium and 650 to 770 of 800 in Large, against 318 and 350 for direct tracking. Only about a quarter of first-query targets lay closer to the goal along free cells, and 15% to 25% lay inside walls in Large. A completion conditioned on the remaining budget spends that budget, so these runs serve as the no-time-cost ablation for section 5. The runs are under `results/pointmaze-closed-loop-dev.J5umEX/`.

**Executor status.** The behavior-cloned executor reached no official goal. A GCIVL executor trained for one million updates reached 0.41 on 20 development resets per task over two seeds, matching OGBench's GCIVL. Tasks 4 and 5 stayed at 0.10 and 0.00, which leaves room for intermediate goals. Scene E1 ran with this executor and a latent-goal GCIVL.

**Scene E1 result (development).** Four world-model seeds, paired with the two executor seeds, on 20 development resets per task. Every arm that pursued model targets stayed at or below direct execution. On Tasks 4 and 5, direct execution reached 0.05 and the arms 0.00 to 0.06. Reusing the same models on Tasks 2 and 3, where direct execution reached 0.625, the arms fell to 0.00 to 0.05. Region goals and point goals did not differ. A probe ruled out the closed loop itself: declaring the official goal as each stage target reproduced direct execution at 0.60 on Task 2, while the model's forecast gave 0.00. The world model predicts better than simple references, with a normalized error of 0.67 against 1.36 for the current observation and 1.05 for linear interpolation toward the goal. Its deterministic forecast, though, averages several possible futures: decoded button states sit between on and off. On held-out windows, the true future 37 steps ahead met the native arrival predicate at the decoded forecast 6% of the time, compared with 9% at the unchanged current observation. Only 6.6% of decoded forecasts matched more goal parts than the current state, against 19% of true futures. Region realizations and flow samples raised that share to 12% and 10.5%. Useful targets therefore appear among sampled candidates but are rare, and pursuing an unselected forecast until the last eight actions leaves no time to finish. This is the case for selecting among candidates with the option value, with the terminal goal kept as a candidate. The runs are under `results/scene-regions-dev.8vx0P9/` and `results/scene-regions-t23-dev.AcFqnA/`.

**Conclusion.** Region goals did not beat point goals in any environment, and every result depended on which target was pursued and when. The method drops the region decoder and arrival tests and chooses latent options with a learned value (section 5).

### 5. Learn budget-conditioned option value in latent space

The confirmed selectors used hand-designed landmark distances and slack in the grid and a public-state transition table in budget routes. E1 added two more hand-set pieces: per-task arrival tests and query offsets at fixed fractions of the remaining budget, which shrank toward the deadline and caused hundreds of replans per episode. The method drops all of them.

An option pursues a latent `z` for at most `k` actions with one latent-goal executor. Its meaning is where pursuing it lands, so no decoder target or arrival test is needed. The value `Q(h, g, z, k, b, λ)` scores a candidate together with its duration, given the terminal goal `g`, the remaining budget `b` and a time cost `λ` per action. It is first fitted offline on hindsight options: each archive segment from `s_t` to `s_{t+k}` pursued `enc(s_{t+k})`, and the encoded relabeled goal is an option pursued for the whole budget. The semi-Markov target is `1 − λd` on reaching the goal after `d` actions, `−λb` when the budget runs out, and `−λk + V(s_{t+k}, g, b − k, λ)` otherwise, with `V` an upper expectile of `Q` over the options the archive pursued. The backups have no discount, so `V` is clipped to the returns still possible, `[−λ(b − k), 1 − λ]`. Without the clip each backup added the expectile's bias, values reached 25 on labeled options, the goal candidate, whose target needs no backup, always lost, and the planner first succeeded within about ten actions of the limit. The archive also assumes every option is reached and never pursued the terminal goal on purpose, so the value is corrected in rounds with the executor's own attempts on training resets. Collection explores uniformly over proposal budgets and also plays every reset by pursuing the terminal goal for the whole budget without interruption, so the goal option is labeled by its own outcome; evaluation keeps interruption. Report terminal success against the number of attempts. Fit a Monte Carlo selector on the same latent features and attempts as the reference.

One value decides where to go, how far and when to change course:

- **Candidates and durations.** At a decision the world model samples latent futures at each query offset, and every sample at every offset is a candidate with that offset as its duration. Offsets should grow geometrically up to the remaining budget, so they set the resolution of durations without tying them to one task; the first milestone reuses the trained fraction offsets. A completion conditioned on the remaining budget paces its routes to the deadline, so the world model also proposes routes under shorter budgets, and the value scores every candidate with the true remaining budget.
- **The terminal goal is a candidate.** The encoded goal, pursued for the remaining budget, competes with the samples. The planner stops looking for intermediate goals when going straight to the goal is worth more, and it can always fall back to direct execution: in Scene, model targets pursued without that option drove success below direct execution.
- **Interruption and on-demand replanning.** Each step the planner re-scores the current option with its remaining duration and the cached candidates, and switches when one beats it by more than the spread of an ensemble of value heads. It samples new candidates only when the duration is spent, the current value drops by more than the spread, or the encoded observation departs from the predicted latent path. Interrupting an option for a better one never lowers the return under an exact value (Sutton, Precup and Singh, 1999).

**Maze2D probe (development, seed 7).** 64 starts per layout with 50,000 executor and value updates and three online rounds of 256 episodes. With the clipped value, the corrected value and shorter-budget proposals together reached 1.00 in Medium, first succeeding at step 148 against 181 for direct latent pursuit, and 0.91 in Large against 0.45, or 82% of the oracle gap. Either change alone was weaker: without shorter-budget proposals Large reached 0.83 but first succeeded at step 758 of 800, and without correction it reached 0.56. In Large, removing interruption cost 0.13 and fixing the duration delayed success to step 711, while removing the goal candidate changed little because the corrected value rarely chose it. The runs are under `results/pointmaze-options-probe.b9NZa4/`.

Two settings control how the value trades speed and risk. The time cost `λ` is a value input, so one value serves every setting: with `λ = 0` only success within the budget counts, and a larger `λ` prefers candidates that finish early. The second is exploration strength: how many candidates the flow proposes, and whether selection ranks by a lower or upper quantile across value heads, for cautious or aggressive choices. The Maze2D E1 runs, in which the budget-conditioned completion reached the goal near the action limit and detoured on the way, are the `λ = 0` ablation without selection. Report terminal success, steps to success, generations, switches and the share of decisions that chose the goal across both settings. [Maze2D option planner](experiments.md#plan-with-latent-options-in-maze2d)

Ablate the planner on the same seeds and starts: without the goal candidate, without interruption, with a fixed duration, with the offline value only, without shorter-budget proposals, without budget conditioning `Q(h, g, z, k)`, with a HiTS-style high level that attaches a duration to each goal and optimizes discounted return, and with replanning at a fixed interval. Add likelihood-based selection in the style of DSP and report the rank correlation between likelihood and value at each budget.

Each online round reports its episodes and environment steps. Evaluate after each round to plot terminal success against attempts, and give two baselines the same environment steps: additional demonstrations for the world model and executor, and fine-tuning of the executor on its own rollouts toward the terminal goal.

**Exit criterion.** On every Maze2D seed and layout, the corrected value with shorter-budget proposals beats direct execution and direct latent pursuit, first succeeds clearly before the action limit, and keeps `Q` within the returns possible. If the matched-interaction baselines catch up, report online correction as a way to spend interactions rather than as a free gain.

**Full development run.** Medium and Large on world-model seeds 7, 19 and 31, 64 starts each, with 300,000 executor updates, 200,000 value updates and three online rounds of 256 episodes. `Q` stayed within the returns possible (largest magnitude 1.08). In Large the corrected value with shorter-budget proposals reached 1.00 on every seed against 0.48 for direct execution, 0.57 for direct latent pursuit and 1.00 for the oracle, first succeeding at step 324. The offline value with shorter-budget proposals already reached 0.97, and without shorter-budget proposals the corrected value first succeeded near the limit (step 727 of 800), so the shorter budgets carry most of the gain. A fixed duration cost the most (0.81, step 653); removing interruption gave 0.97 and removing the goal candidate changed nothing. Medium is at ceiling: direct latent pursuit already reached 0.995. The runs are under `results/options-dev.ygAGOa/`.

### 6. Run the option planner in Scene

Scene tests whether the planner helps where direct execution already succeeds (Tasks 2 and 3, about 0.6) and where it almost never does (Tasks 4 and 5, about 0.05), and whether the terminal-goal candidate keeps it at or above direct execution where unselected targets fell below it in section 4. `wr scene-options` reuses the world model and latent-goal executor of each E1 seed, labels hindsight options with the official scene predicate, and corrects the value on training resets of Tasks 2 to 5 with seeds disjoint from development and test. The arms match Maze2D without an oracle. [Scene option planner](experiments.md#plan-with-latent-options-in-scene)

**Exit criterion.** On Tasks 2 and 3 the planner stays at or above direct execution on every seed. On Tasks 4 and 5 it raises success over direct execution, and the chosen options can be inspected for an open drawer.

**Full development run.** Seeds 7, 19, 31 and 43, 20 starts per task. The corrected value with shorter-budget proposals reached 0.42 against 0.34 for direct execution and 0.36 for direct latent pursuit (+0.06 to +0.11 per seed, intervals touching zero). It raised Task 4 from 0.10 to 0.34 and Task 5 from 0.00 to 0.31 but lowered Tasks 2 and 3 from 0.63 to 0.53 and 0.51; pooled over seeds, 25 and 24 starts were solved only by direct latent pursuit against 10 and 14 only by the planner. The planner's success was nearly flat across tasks because it chose the goal in 2% of decisions. At the first decision the value put the goal near zero on Tasks 2 and 3 (offline 0.03 and 0.15, corrected −0.01 and 0.08) while direct latent pursuit succeeded 0.71 and 0.64 of the time, and it barely ranked starts by goal success (AUC 0.61 and 0.56). The chosen sampled option was calibrated (0.54 predicted, 0.53 realized on Task 2; AUC 0.76). The goal was underrated before correction because offline goal options are labeled by whether the demonstrator reached the goal: relabeled goals come from the start's own trajectory, and random goals from other trajectories are labeled failures. At the full budget and similar latent distances, the offline value gave 0.74 to goals 100 to 400 steps ahead in the same trajectory and 0.21 to goals from other trajectories, and task goals, which never share a trajectory with the start, fell with the latter. Correction could not fix it because exploration switched away from the goal after a median of one to four steps, so no attempt labeled the goal pursued for the whole budget. Collection now adds uninterrupted goal pursuit on every reset. An evaluation-only rerun with the same values reproduced all 8,128 episodes and showed the goal's value below the best sampled option at every remaining budget, not only at the first decision.

### 7. Compare with Diffuser and HIQL in Maze2D

Run Diffuser and HIQL on the same environment versions, dataset, resets and action budgets as the option planner, in Medium and Large, with budgets from 300 to 600 in Medium. Keep direct execution, direct latent pursuit and the oracle on the same starts. The earlier frozen-energy pilot found useful candidates for 15 and 10 of 41 direct failures while minimum energy tied the highest-energy rule. [Energy pilot](experiments.md#test-latent-energy-against-terminal-success)

**Exit criterion.** Paired terminal success and first success step by budget for every method, and the budgets at which the planner's chosen first option changes.

### 8. Compare supervision sources on a shared bank

This comparison isolates the label that trains the selector. On frozen candidate banks with the same features, executor and training attempts, compare the option value with a trajectory-labeled energy in the style of HDFlow, a reachability filter with continuation value in the style of HECRL, arrival only, route length in the style of HorizonFlow, endpoint distance in the style of Fast-JEPA, frozen energy and uniform choice. `wr attempt-compare` already implements the Monte Carlo attempt value, the factorized filter and arrival only on `shared_attempts_v1` banks. [Selector comparison](experiments.md#compare-selectors-on-matched-attempts)

**Exit criterion.** Paired terminal success with world-bootstrap intervals, arrival and miss-then-success rates, and a prespecified analysis of disagreements, such as candidates the factorized filter rejects although their attempts often recover after a miss.

### 9. Scale to deeper tasks

Extend the dependency grid to three and four ordered prerequisites at fixed map size, where enumerating ordered goals becomes infeasible. Compare the option planner with enumeration plus dynamic programming where it is feasible and with direct terminal-latent scoring in the style of Fast-JEPA, at matched model calls.

**Exit criterion.** Paired success by depth with uncertainty, with candidate counts, model calls and latency reported separately.

### 10. Isolate the representation contribution

Compare the frozen reconstruction encoder with an end-to-end predictive encoder regularized by SIGReg. Hold the planner, data, executor and value fixed and match capacity where possible. Track embedding covariance or effective rank as collapse diagnostics, probe whether the representation distinguishes histories with different inventory, lock state or grasp status, and report terminal success. Until this comparison is done, describe the method as masked latent prediction with a frozen reconstruction codec rather than as a JEPA representation.

### 11. Vary demonstration coverage

In budget routes, keep every local segment in the demonstrations and set the frequency of the complete reset-to-terminal route to 0%, 5% and 20% at a fixed demonstration count. Add at least two further graph topologies with different numbers of resources and gates. Freeze the support catalogue on training episodes before proposing candidates.

**Exit criterion.** The composition gain over joint sampling and replay, and the number of independently confirmed low-support paths, as functions of route coverage on each topology.

## Reporting

The primary outcome is the paired change in terminal success under a fixed action budget, with the first success step. Report it across starts, layouts and training seeds. Record candidate generations, switches, decisions by trigger, the share of decisions that chose the terminal goal, the chosen duration against the remaining budget and the range of the value as diagnostics. Choose settings and analysis rules on development resets and freeze them before the test split. Meter environment interactions for training, online correction and evaluation separately. Keep snapshots, simulator random streams, privileged planners and test outcomes on the evaluator side of the interface. The team's local research protocol, `local-file/research_idea/research-protocol.md`, gives the full rules.
