# Research plan for discovering useful latent subgoals

**Can a world model discover regions in latent space that improve final task success when pursued?** The planner receives the public observation and action history, the terminal goal, and the remaining action budget. It learns from trajectories without hand-labeled subgoals. The terminal objective can admit several valid routes or outcomes.

An intermediate goal is a latent vector `z` produced by the world model. Its meaning is the conditional distribution `p(s | z)` of concrete states that realize it, so `z` names a region of state space rather than a single target. Before decoding, nobody knows what the goal is. The executor pursues `z` directly and judges arrival in latent space, and the decoder reveals afterwards what the model discovered.

The value of a goal region `z` is the probability of terminal success after assigning `z` to the shared executor, pursuing it until arrival, deadline or termination, and continuing with the shared policy on the actual remaining budget. The advantage of `z` is its value minus the value of direct execution. Success after a missed goal counts. This definition includes the time spent pursuing the goal and the chance of recovering after a miss.

## Hypotheses

1. **Goals as regions.** A latent goal whose realizations are sampled from `p(s | z)` is coherent when its realizations have similar attempt value. Pursuing such a region gives the executor several ways to satisfy the goal and raises both arrival and terminal success over pursuing a single decoded state. Region granularity trades arrival against downstream value.
2. **Goal-dependent value.** From a matched start, pursuing some regions or ordered sequences improves terminal success under a shared executor and action budget. Changing the terminal goal can change which regions help.
3. **Plausibility differs from value.** A pretrained world model can propose useful regions, but its frozen energy, its prediction error and the demonstration frequency of a goal identify plausible futures rather than valuable ones. An option value learned from attempts identifies the valuable ones before rollout. Supervision from attempts also differs from supervision from recorded trajectory outcomes, arrival alone, route length or distance to a goal example.
4. **Budget-dependent composition.** Separately proposed regions can be recombined into ordered routes. Scoring those routes with a budget-conditioned value finds reliable routes that joint samples and replay rarely contain, and the chosen route changes with the budget.
5. **Task depth.** As the number of dependent decisions grows, enumerating ordered goals becomes infeasible and predicting a distant latent endpoint may become less reliable for ranking plans. Test whether value-guided generation of region sequences preserves useful rankings and improves closed-loop success across task depths. Define depth by prerequisite decisions and log path length, action budget, and inference horizon separately.

## Method

| Component | Design |
| --- | --- |
| Latent goal proposer | The masked completion model predicts `ẑ(h, g, b, q)` at query offset `q` from the public history `h`, terminal condition `g` and remaining budget `b`. Candidate goals come from several query offsets and, where needed, residual flow samples around the prediction. |
| Region decoder | A conditional diffusion or flow decoder models `p(s | z)`. It is trained on pairs of predicted latents and the true future states at the queried offset. A point prediction regressed toward the mean of several futures can lie off the encoder's manifold, so the decoder learns the realizations that follow from the predicted latent itself. |
| Latent-conditioned executor | `π(a | h, z)` is trained on demonstrations with latent goals at matched offsets, preferably the predicted latents so that training and deployment inputs agree. It never receives a decoded or projected target. |
| Arrival in latent space | Encoder consistency declares arrival when `‖enc(s) − z‖ ≤ ε`. Region support declares arrival when `log p(s | z) ≥ κ`. Encoder consistency is cheap but can fail when `z` lies between modes. Region support follows the decoder's realizations but costs a likelihood estimate. Both are calibrated on development data and compared in experiment 4. |
| Option value | `Q(h, z, b) = E[V(h', b − τ)]`, where the attempt lasts `τ` actions and lands at history `h'` whether or not it reached the region, and `V(h, b) = max(Q_direct(h, b), max_z Q(h, z, b))`. At the final decision `V` is the terminal success probability. The value head reads latent features: the encoded history, the candidate latent, the predicted latent at the query time, the budget and the query offset. Bellman backups need only single-segment attempts, and the Monte Carlo selector fitted on complete attempts serves as the reference. |
| Region sequences | Plan ordered sequences of regions with the learned `V`. At depth two, enumerate candidate pairs and run dynamic programming. At larger depths, generate sequences with value guidance and compare with enumeration at matched inference budgets. |

The current implementation decodes each latent deterministically and projects it to a cell, a graph node or an observation for observation-conditioned executors. Experiments 1 to 3 below used that pipeline. Experiment 4 introduces region goals.

## Positioning against prior work

No single component of the method is new. DisCo RL already represents a goal as a distribution over states, conditions the policy on its parameters and rewards log-likelihood under it. Goal-Space Planning, STAR and GARA already treat subgoals as sets of states with subgoal-level values. SAGE, HWM, H-JEPA and Director already predict or select latent subgoals for a latent-conditioned executor. HiTS already runs a subgoal until its deadline and continues after a miss. Horizon-aware C-Learning and TDM already learn horizon-conditioned reaching probabilities with Bellman recursions. HDFlow and Hierarchical Diffuser already guide subgoal-sequence diffusion with a learned score. RCDM already interprets self-supervised representations with a conditional diffusion decoder. The contribution is the combination that none of them makes: a world model predicts the goal region from the current history, terminal goal and budget, and the region is valued by what attempting it does to terminal success under that budget.

| Claim | Nearest work | Difference and distinguishing evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A latent goal names a region defined by `p(s | z)`, predicted from context, and value belongs to the region. | DisCo RL fits simple goal distributions to supplied examples and chains hand-specified subtasks. Goal-Space Planning, STAR and GARA use fixed partitions of the state space. SAGE, HWM, H-JEPA and Director use single latent points. | Our regions are predicted from `(h, g, b)`, differ by context, may overlap, and are realized by an expressive learned decoder. Section 4 compares them with single latent points under latent-distance arrival and with a Gaussian region in the style of DisCo RL, and tests region coherence and granularity. |
| Goal value is the terminal success probability of attempting a region under the global remaining budget, including recovery after a miss. | HiTS attaches a duration to each subgoal and optimizes discounted return. Horizon-aware C-Learning and TDM learn horizon-conditioned values for reaching single goals with primitive actions. HDFlow uses an energy trained on successful and failed trajectories. HECRL filters by reachability, then ranks by value from the achieved subgoal. | The option value generalizes horizon-aware reaching probabilities to region options and the global budget. Section 5 ablates recovery, budget conditioning and a HiTS-style discounted high level. Section 6 compares supervision sources on a shared bank. |
| Plausibility is not value. | DSP argues for selecting subgoals with data-driven generation instead of noisy values. Energy-based planning ranks candidates by model energy. | Frozen energy ties uniform choice while the same pool contains much better candidates (section 2). Sections 5 and 6 add likelihood-based selection in the style of DSP and report rank correlation between likelihood and value across budgets. |
| The budget changes which goal is worth reaching. | HorizonFlow prefers shorter routes. Fast-JEPA ranks by endpoint distance to the goal and uses deadlines to test execution. | One fixed pool selects different routes at different budgets on every test world (section 3). |
| Recombining separately proposed goals finds reliable routes that demonstrations rarely contain. | CTA, SSD and CompDiffuser stitch fragments without separating route support from component support. | Composition confirms more low-support paths with independent repeats than joint sampling or replay (sections 3 and 11). Value-guided sequence generation is an optional generator rather than a core contribution (section 9). |
| Matched interventions with predeclared candidates make the comparison causal. | Prior work reports end-to-end success of complete planners. | Candidate banks, common random numbers and world-level intervals in every comparison. |

Reviewers will also ask for continuous-control evidence (sections 7 and 8) and for the number of simulator attempts the value consumes (section 5).

## Starting evidence

- In the 391-action Maze2D Medium pilot, direct tracking succeeded on 23 of 64 starts. Among its 41 failures, retrospective inspection found at least one candidate that arrived and then succeeded on 15 and 10 starts for the two checkpoints. Selecting the lowest conditional flow energy before rollout yielded 25 and 18 successes, respectively, tying the highest-energy rule within each checkpoint. Better decoded trajectory error and candidate coverage motivate further testing of selection. [Source and protocol](experiments.md#test-latent-energy-against-terminal-success)

### 1. Establish a controlled multi-stage benchmark

Use the dependency grid. Two destinations share the same start, and routes require either one or two keys and gates. Some keys lie on a direct route, and others require a detour. The two-depth comparison keeps map geometry and action budget fixed for each layout. Random action slips are paired across branches and goal switches. Use layout-disjoint splits. After verifying the benchmark, collect trajectories that cover the individual skills and reserve some combinations of skills and layouts for separate generalization tests. [Run the diagnostic](experiments.md#compare-ordered-targets-in-the-dependency-grid)

Use predeclared key locations as diagnostic targets. Compare direct tracking, individual targets, and both orders of the target pair. Record whether a candidate that helps reach one terminal goal loses its advantage when the goal changes. A full-state planner can help verify benchmark solvability. An executor following public geometry provides a local control for the matched attempts.

**Exit criterion.** The public runner reproduces arrival, timeout, recovery, and final-action success. Matched attempts expose goal-dependent effects and order-dependent effects at depth two. Verify the effect on development layouts before training a selector.

The initial CPU run used 16 development layouts, both goals, and 16 paired repeats per start. At depth two, swapping the goal reversed the ranking of the two lane-specific ordered targets on all 16 layouts. The goal-matched entry-then-inner sequence succeeded on 511 of 512 attempts, compared with 199 of 512 for the reversed sequence and 112 of 512 for local direct tracking. These results check the task design with predeclared targets. The run is recorded under `results/dependency-grid-dev-02/`.

### 2. Separate discovery, selection, and execution

Train the existing masked latent predictor on public trajectories as the first representation baseline. Propose goals from latent trajectory completion and compare with matched-time replay targets and geometric or observation-space candidates. Use the same candidate counts, target deadlines, executor, and budget. Include a diagnostic with executable targets from recorded trajectories to measure controller competence.

Declare candidates and scores before rollout. From each start, run the same controller toward each candidate until arrival, timeout, or native termination. Use the same continuation on the observed state with the actual remaining budget. Count terminal successes after misses. Compare no intermediate target, uniform candidate selection, lowest frozen energy, lowest latent prediction error, and matched arrival or proximity controls on the same pool. Use reversed energy as a score direction check. Assess retrospective candidate capacity separately, with independent repeat pools to select and evaluate the reference in stochastic conditions.

**Exit criterion.** Measure both candidate coverage and the gain achieved by prospective selection on held-out worlds. If the pool has useful targets and frozen ranking leaves a clear gap, compare outcome-based scoring learned from training branches with the frozen rules. If coverage is weak, inspect achieved-target execution and compare alternative proposal sources before refining a scorer.

The development comparison used two training seeds with identical demonstrations, 16 layouts, both goals, eight proposals per source, and 16 repeats per reference and evaluation bank. At depth two, uniform flow candidates succeeded on 28.8% and 33.2% of attempts. Minimum energy selected candidates with 28.1% and 34.4% success. Both paired intervals for the energy gain over uniform include zero. Choosing with independent reference repeats exposed more capacity in the flow pools at 46.5% and 62.5%. Matched-time training replay reached 64.8% under the same reference rule and 37.1% when selecting the nearest-layout candidate before execution. The evaluator's replay of timed targets achieved on the development layout reached 59.2%. This comparison motivates retaining replay in both the proposal and scoring experiments. The final test layouts are reserved for the confirmatory protocol. [Run the latent comparison](experiments.md#test-frozen-latent-goals-in-the-dependency-grid)

The fixed-demonstration summaries are under `results/dependency-grid-latent-dev-seed7-02/` and `results/dependency-grid-latent-dev-seed19-02/`.

Fit the same terminal-success scorer for both proposal pools. A training decision observes the public start, goal and budget, assigns one prefix or direct execution, and receives the final binary outcome. Keep the world model and low-level executor frozen. Use repeated training-layout attempts to estimate this single-choice value and freeze the scores before evaluation. Compare the results against uniform selection, frozen energy, nearest replay, and the diagnostic best-of-bank references. A later closed-loop study can learn another choice after observing arrival, timeout, or a changed state. [Run the outcome-based comparison](experiments.md#learn-to-select-a-goal-from-terminal-outcomes)

For the single-choice confirmation, use the existing split, collection seed 7, model seeds 7 and 19, evaluation seed 43301, eight candidates per pool, 16 repeats per training, reference, and evaluation bank, and a 10% action-slip rate. Keep the model update budgets and training procedure from the development runs. The primary contrast at depth two is outcome scoring versus frozen minimum-energy scoring on the same latent pool. The comparison of latent versus replay pools under the shared scorer measures the proposal source effect. Report both model seeds and paired intervals that resample whole layouts. Use the test outcomes to report this fixed comparison.

On the 16 test layouts, outcome scoring raised two-stage latent success from 37.50% to 56.25% for seed 7 and from 43.75% to 68.75% for seed 19. The paired gains were 18.75 and 25.00 percentage points, with 95% layout-bootstrap intervals of [6.25, 34.38] and [9.38, 43.75]. Using the same scorer with replay yielded 62.30% and 59.38%. The latent-minus-replay differences were −6.05 and +9.38 points, and both intervals included zero. This establishes a benefit from outcome-based ranking in the dependency grid. The experiment used public landmark geometry, a fixed continuation, and one high-level decision. ### 3. Compose alternative routes under a budget

The central case is a single start and terminal objective with several feasible routes whose value depends on resources, uncertainty and time. Generate latent goals from public histories. Use a model of controlled transitions to score possible target orders by the chance of terminal success under the actual budget. Compare the original joint samples, recombined goals, individual targets, and matched-context replay with one executor and a common proposal cap. Reuse a fixed candidate bank across budgets to isolate selection from changing proposals.

Use the budgeted-routes task to separate rare combinations from unfamiliar individual targets. Include the local segments in training and omit the full reset-to-terminal route from the scripted demonstration distribution. Record ordered and component support separately. Screen each proposed low-support execution path on independent reference repeats, then estimate its reliability and paired terminal gain on fresh repeats. The controlled comparison retains a public-state transition model for scoring and a learned latent model for proposing targets. [Run the experiment](experiments.md#compose-latent-goals-under-action-budgets)

For the initial confirmation, freeze the development configuration and use seeds 7 and 19 on the eight test worlds. Keep the collection, proposal and evaluation seeds fixed, the two query offsets, the 24-candidate cap, 64 repeats per reference and evaluation bank, and the budgets of 2, 3 and 4 actions. The primary comparison is composition versus joint sampling and replay at four actions under the common scorer. Report paired success intervals by world and coverage of independently verified rare paths. The fixed-pool route changes diagnose budget conditioning. Suffix replanning is a separate comparison with its own query count.

On the shared eight test worlds, the fixed-pool scorer changed its selected route across budgets on every world for both model seeds. At four actions, composition achieved 93.16% terminal success. Joint sampling, single targets, and matched-context replay each achieved 84.38%. The paired gain over joint sampling and replay was 8.79 percentage points for both seeds, with a world-bootstrap interval of [1.17, 16.80]. Independent repeats confirmed 12 low-support world–path pairs per seed for composition, versus zero and three for joint sampling and zero for replay. Replanning after the observed first target raised success to 95.70%, with a paired gain of 2.54 points [0.39, 5.66]. On the four worlds where the planner selected a pair, supply then gate achieved 88.67%, versus 62.89% in reverse order, 71.09% for supply alone, and 25.78% for gate alone. This study uses one graph topology, scripted demonstration support, and a public-state transition scorer. Runs with the paired order audit are recorded in `results/budget-routes-test-seed7-02/` and `results/budget-routes-test-seed19-02/`.

### 4. Compare region goals with decoded point goals

This experiment tests the core claim in the two controlled tasks. Freeze the world model and proposals from the confirmation runs. Train the region decoder on pairs of predicted latents and true future observations from training layouts. Train the latent-conditioned executor on demonstrations with latent goals at matched offsets. Keep the continuation, deadlines, budgets and paired slips of the earlier runs.

| Arm | Goal given to the executor and arrival rule |
| --- | --- |
| Decoded point goal | Decode `z` once, project it to a cell or node, and require exact arrival. Run it with the public shortest-path controller and with a learned observation-conditioned executor. |
| Latent point, in the style of SAGE and HWM | Condition the latent executor on `z` and declare arrival when `‖enc(s) − z‖ ≤ ε`. This is the encoder-consistency rule and uses no region decoder. |
| Region goal, region support | Condition the latent executor on `z` and declare arrival when the kernel density of `s` under samples from `p(s | z)` reaches κ. |
| Gaussian region, in the style of DisCo RL | Match a diagonal Gaussian to the same decoder samples and use its support for arrival. |
| Region goal, decoded executor | Condition the observation executor on one sample from `p(s | z)` per repeat, with region-support arrival. This separates the effect of the region from the effect of the latent-conditioned executor. |

Both executors share an architecture and are trained by behavior cloning on the same demonstrations, so the comparison does not reward a hand-written controller. The continuation after the stages is shared.

Calibrate `ε` and `κ` on development windows so that each rule accepts the same share of realized states, at 25%, 50% and 75%, and report the false arrival rate on mismatched states. Rules are then compared at equal recall, and the sweep traces region granularity against arrival and value. Measure region coherence by sampling several realizations from `p(s | z)`, attempting each as a point goal on matched streams, and reporting the spread of attempt value within a region against the spread between regions. Decode the regions with the highest and lowest learned value on held-out layouts and check whether they correspond to key, gate or supply states.

**Exit criterion.** Report arrival, value of attempting and paired terminal success by arm on test layouts with layout-bootstrap intervals. Region goals support the claim when they raise terminal success over point goals on the same proposals and their realizations agree in value more than realizations of different regions do. Choose the arrival rule for later experiments from the development results.

**Development result in the dependency grid (provisional).** Five model seeds on the 16 development layouts, averaging terminal success over the eight flow candidates per start, with 95% intervals that resample layouts after averaging seeds. Judging arrival on the whole observation instead of the decoded cell gave the largest effect. With the same observation executor and decoded target, region-support arrival beat exact-cell arrival by 0.21 [0.15, 0.29] at depth one and by 0.16 [0.12, 0.21] at depth two at recall 0.9, on every seed. Exact-cell arrival ignores the key flags, so it hands control to the key-blind continuation before the key is collected. Under region-support arrival, a sampled realization beat the decoded mean by 0.02 to 0.03 on four of five seeds. At depth two the flow decoder beat the matched Gaussian by 0.01 to 0.05. Region support did not beat encoder consistency at equal recall: at recall 0.9 encoder consistency was ahead by 0.024 [0.014, 0.034] at depth two. Regions were not coherent: the variance of value across realizations of one region (0.085 at depth two) exceeded the variance between regions of the same start (0.058). Depth-two success rose with recall up to 0.9 and fell slightly at 0.95. The learned executor already encodes the demonstrated key-fetching behavior, so depth one barely depends on the arrival rule. The grid therefore cannot separate goal forms well. The next tests move to Maze2D and Scene. The runs are under `results/dependency-grid-regions-dev.Ya1JrG/`.

**Maze2D one-shot run (withdrawn as evidence).** The first Maze2D run declared one target per start and then tracked the terminal goal. Replacing the model's target with a perfect shortest-path waypoint raised success by at most 0.03 over direct tracking, while dense shortest-path tracking succeeded on all 64 starts in Medium and Large. The protocol therefore could not separate goal forms, and its result says nothing about regions. The models were also small: 20% to 54% of their first targets lay inside walls. E1 in Maze2D now plans in closed loop between a direct floor and a shortest-path ceiling, on world models trained at a larger scale. On 16 Medium starts with the original model, decoded and sampled targets under the tracker reached 0.72 to 1.00 against 0.50 for direct tracking and 1.00 for the oracle, which shows the room the protocol now has. The latent executor arms stayed between 0.19 and 0.50. The one-shot runs are under `results/pointmaze-regions-dev.mPhg3O/`.

### 5. Learn budget-conditioned option value in latent space

The confirmed selectors used hand-designed landmark distances and slack in the grid and a public-state transition table in budget routes. Both encode task knowledge the world model should discover, so the method drops them and estimates value only from latent features with the option value `Q(h, z, b)`. Collect single-segment training attempts that record the region, deadline, elapsed time, landing history and arrival. Fit `Q` by fitted Q iteration with the semi-Markov backup, and use the terminal outcome at the final decision. For routes, the same value composes ordered regions through `V(h, b)`. Fit a Monte Carlo selector on the same latent features and complete attempts from the same training layouts as the reference.

Ablate the value definition on the same attempts: a value that counts only success after arrival, a value without budget conditioning `Q(h, z)`, and a HiTS-style high level that attaches a duration to each goal and optimizes discounted return. Add likelihood-based selection in the style of DSP and report the rank correlation between likelihood and value at each budget.

Count the simulator attempts each selector consumes and report them in every table. Refit both selectors on 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 training attempts per candidate and plot test terminal success against attempts. Give two baselines the same number of environment steps: additional demonstrations for the proposer and executor, and fine-tuning of the executor on its own rollouts toward the terminal goal.

**Exit criterion.** The latent option value beats frozen energy and closes most of the gap to the reference-selected candidate, with paired intervals on the same test banks. The curve shows how many attempts each selector needs to beat frozen energy and the matched-interaction baselines. If the baselines catch up at the same budget, report the value as a way to spend interactions rather than as a free gain. If the latent value falls well short, probe which public variables the latent features fail to encode and relate the result to section 10.

### 6. Compare supervision sources on a shared bank

This comparison isolates the label that trains the selector. Freeze the region-goal banks from section 4. Every arm sees the same candidates, features, executor, continuation and training attempts, and chooses one candidate or direct execution per start before evaluation.

| Arm | Training signal and decision rule |
| --- | --- |
| Option value | Section 5. Choose the highest value and keep direct execution unless a candidate is strictly better. |
| Trajectory-labeled energy, as in HDFlow | Fit a contrastive energy on latent goals extracted from successful and failed recorded trajectories at matched times. The labels come from whole trajectories, not from attempts of the candidate. Choose the lowest energy. |
| Factorized filter, as in HECRL | Estimate arrival and the value of continuing from the achieved goal. Filter by an arrival threshold set on development layouts, then rank by continuation value. |
| Arrival only | Choose the candidate with the highest predicted arrival. |
| Route length, as in HorizonFlow | Choose the shortest candidate route that the proposer generated. |
| Endpoint distance, as in Fast-JEPA | Predict the terminal latent after the attempt and choose the smallest distance to the encoded terminal condition. |
| Frozen energy and uniform choice | The existing reference arms. |

`wr attempt-compare` already implements the Monte Carlo attempt value, factorized filter and arrival-only arms on `shared_attempts_v1` banks. Export the region-goal attempts in that format and add the remaining arms. Fit every learned arm with the same model class and tuning budget on training layouts and select thresholds on development layouts.

**Exit criterion.** Report paired terminal success on test layouts with world-bootstrap intervals, together with arrival, miss-then-success and the share of starts where each pair of arms disagrees. A gain of the option value over the trajectory-labeled energy and the factorized filter on the same bank attributes the improvement to the label. Prespecify the disagreement analysis: cases where the factorized filter rejects a region whose attempts often recover after a miss, and cases where a frequent trajectory goal attempts poorly.

Run the terminal comparison on official Tasks 4 and 5 after qualifying the latent-conditioned executor.

The observation-conditioned executor reached insertion within 128 actions on 9 to 39 of 108 development clips across six scales and serves as the reference. The terminal comparison runs on official resets with these arms: direct execution, minimum frozen energy on region goals, option-value selection on region goals, matched-time replay encoded as region goals with the same value, and targets extracted at matched times from Diffuser or Hierarchical Diffuser and handed to the observation-conditioned executor. Fit the value on training resets from separate seeds and report terminal success with intervals across starts and training seeds. Decode the selected regions and report how often they show an open drawer.

**Exit criterion.** Proceed to the terminal comparison only when insertion arrival passes a threshold fixed on development clips and direct terminal success on development resets is neither near zero nor near one.

### 8. Compare with Diffuser in Maze2D

Run our planner and Diffuser on the same environment versions, dataset, resets and action budgets. Sweep budgets from 300 to 600 in Medium and include Large. Add region goals and the option value to the existing frozen-energy comparison and keep direct execution, minimum energy and uniform choice on the same candidate banks.

**Exit criterion.** Report paired terminal success by budget for every arm and the budgets at which the selected first goal changes. The pilot found useful candidates in the pool for 15 and 10 of 41 direct failures, while minimum energy tied the highest-energy rule. A gain of the option value over minimum energy at matched budgets would reproduce the grid result on a standard benchmark.

### 9. Generate region sequences as depth grows

Extend the dependency grid to three and four ordered prerequisites at fixed map size. Compare three planners with the same proposer, executor, option value and primitive-action budget: enumeration of ordered regions with dynamic programming, generation of region sequences guided by the learned value, and a direct terminal-latent scoring planner in the style of Fast-JEPA. Measure how each score ranks actual outcomes as task depth grows. In the end-to-end comparison, report success across matched inference budgets, with candidate counts, model calls, and latency measured separately.

**Exit criterion.** Estimate the paired success-rate difference by depth with uncertainty and report candidate ranking at the same depths. Guided generation earns its place when it matches enumeration at depth two and keeps its success at depths where enumeration exceeds the inference budget.

### 10. Isolate the representation contribution

Compare the current frozen reconstruction encoder with an end-to-end predictive encoder regularized by SIGReg. Hold the planner, data, region decoder, executor and value fixed and match capacity where possible. Track embedding covariance or effective rank as collapse diagnostics. Probe whether the representation distinguishes public histories with different inventory, lock state or grasp status, and whether those distinctions predict future feasibility. Report region coherence and the paired terminal-success endpoint. Add a within-architecture regularizer ablation to attribute changes specifically to SIGReg. Until this comparison is done, describe the method as masked latent prediction with a frozen reconstruction codec rather than as a JEPA representation.

### 11. Vary demonstration coverage

In budget routes, keep every local segment in the demonstrations and set the frequency of the complete reset-to-terminal route to 0%, 5% and 20% at a fixed demonstration count. Add at least two further graph topologies with different numbers of resources and gates. Freeze the support catalogue on training episodes before proposing candidates.

**Exit criterion.** Report the composition gain over joint sampling and replay, and the number of independently confirmed low-support paths, as functions of route coverage on each topology. A gain that shrinks as coverage rises would show that composition matters where demonstrations are sparse.

### 12. Plan several decisions in closed loop

The confirmed results use one high-level decision per start, plus one replanning step in budget routes. Let the planner choose a new region after each arrival, timeout or detected change in the public state. The option value already bootstraps across decision points, so train it on attempts collected at later decisions as well. Compare event-triggered replanning with replanning at a fixed interval and with the single-decision planner at matched model calls and action budget.

**Exit criterion.** Report terminal success, the number of decisions per episode, and model calls. A gain over the single-decision planner at matched calls supports the sequential use of the option value.

## Reporting

The primary outcome is the paired change in terminal success under a fixed action budget. Report it across held-out layouts, task depths, and training seeds, with uncertainty clustered by layout. Record arrival, attempt time, missed-target recoveries, region coherence, goal-switch rankings, and candidate coverage as diagnostics. Choose practical effect thresholds, arrival thresholds and analysis rules on development data, then freeze them before confirmation. Meter environment interactions used for training, selector fitting, feedback, and evaluation separately. Keep snapshots, simulator random streams, reference rankings, and test outcomes on the evaluator side of the interface.
